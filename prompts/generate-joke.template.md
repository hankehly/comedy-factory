# Generate Joke

Your task is to write a joke by filtering the subtext below through one or more of the funny filters described in this document.

## Rules

* Replace this list with your own rules for what makes a good joke.

## Output

Set `text` to the joke and nothing else — no preamble, no explanation, no surrounding quotation marks.

Set `rationale` to a short description of how the joke was created: which funny filter(s) you used and how the joke reveals the subtext to the reader.

## Choosing Filters

Choose the filter(s) that best fit the subtext. Optionally add your own guidance here on when each filter works best.

## Funny Filters

The eleven funny filters below come from Scott Dikkers, founding editor of The Onion ([source](https://bigthink.com/high-culture/every-joke-falls-in-one-of-these-11-categories-according-to-the-founder-of-the-onion/)). Optionally enrich any filter — or delete the ones you don't use — with more detail: how to use it, a longer description, and example jokes (for instance with `#### What it is`, `#### How to use it`, `#### Description`, and `#### Examples` subsections).

### 1. Irony

Expressing the opposite of the intended meaning.

### 2. Character

Humor from a persona acting on its clearly defined traits.

### 3. Reference

Calling out a shared experience the audience recognizes as true.

### 4. Shock

Anything shocking: sex, violence, swearing, or a gross-out.

### 5. Parody

Imitating a familiar form or genre and subverting it.

### 6. Hyperbole

Exaggeration beyond the bounds of science or reason.

### 7. Wordplay

Puns, double meanings, and funny-sounding words.

### 8. Analogy

Comparing two seemingly unrelated things to comedic effect.

### 9. Madcap

Zany, absurd, over-the-top silliness.

### 10. Misplaced Focus

Directing attention to the wrong thing.

### 11. Metahumor

Jokes about jokes or comedy itself.
